# Relay design research: cited decisions (M4.1)

Relay should take its shape, spacing, type and motion tokens straight from each platform's own published system (Windows 11 geometry and Segoe UI Variable on the PC; Material 3 tokens on Android). It should treat glass as a near-opaque, optional surface. No vendor publishes opacity, blur or contrast numbers for glass that carry text, so every glass number below is either borrowed from a library port or derived by us. Our derivation shows a neutral scrim needs about **0.58 alpha for 4.5:1 text and 0.45 for 3:1 icons** against a worst-case opposite backdrop, so Relay uses **0.70+ for text surfaces and 0.50+ for icon-only surfaces**, with a solid fallback. Radii, spacing, type, easing and the Android springs are published and high confidence. Windows springs, border alpha, highlight and the amber role are decisions without a published number. Amber becomes a **warning role, never a second accent**. WCAG 1.4.11 holds a component outline to 3:1 only when the outline is the sole cue that identifies the control; hairline dividers are exempt. Many primary pages (m3.material.io, Apple HIG, Fluent 2 sub-pages, NN/g) could not be fetched, and several reference apps were listed from memory; both are flagged below.

Legend. **P** = published value read from a source. **D** = derived by the researchers (WCAG formula, spring math). **C** = our design choice. **U** = source unverified or secondary.

## Glass is a scrim problem: opacity first, scrim second, darker text last

Windows builds acrylic from five layers (background, blur, exclusion blend, tint/luminosity overlay, noise) and keeps text legible with a luminosity layer at 0.85 to 0.96, which means the surface is almost opaque ([Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/acrylic)). Microsoft's pages give no defaults; the numbers come from Haze's Fluent port, which the researchers took to mirror WinUI but did not check against WinUI source: dark container #2C2C2C, tint 0.15, luminosity 0.96; light #FCFCFC, tint 0, luminosity 0.85; blur 60 dp; noise 0.02 ([Haze FluentMaterials.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chrisbanes/haze/main/haze-materials/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/chrisbanes/haze/blur/materials/FluentMaterials.kt)). Haze's Material-style presets use a fixed 24 dp blur and vary only tint alpha, from 0.35 (ultraThin, light) to 0.97 (ultraThick, dark) ([Haze HazeMaterials.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chrisbanes/haze/main/haze-materials/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/chrisbanes/haze/blur/materials/HazeMaterials.kt)). Apple publishes no numbers either: its answer is adaptive glyph flipping, a dimming layer under the Clear variant, and, since 26.1, a Tinted mode that "increases opacity and adds more contrast" ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-liquid-glass-toggle/)). NN/g found translucent controls "often invisible" and text over images illegible ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Every vendor's shipped fix is therefore more opacity, which agrees with our math.

The derived worst-case minimum alphas (neutral surface over a same-colour-as-text backdrop, blur assumed to flatten the backdrop) are: black surface with white text 0.417 / 0.535 / 0.651 for 3:1 / 4.5:1 / 7:1; #121212 surface 0.448 / 0.576 / 0.701; white surface with black text 0.350 / 0.456 / 0.584. Haze alpha 0.35 over white gives only 2.44:1, while 0.60 gives 5.74:1 and 0.73 gives 9.61:1 (all D). The flat-backdrop assumption understates large bright or dark regions that a 24 dp blur cannot flatten, so these are floors, and the margins below are ours.

Of the three techniques, opacity is the primary lever and a separate scrim is the same lever in another form (Apple's dimming layer, Microsoft's luminosity layer). Use an explicit scrim only behind modal sheets and consent dialogs, where Microsoft's Smoke layer and Apple's dimming guidance agree ([Materials](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/materials)). Darker or lighter text cannot rescue a low-alpha surface because a pure colour is already the ceiling; secondary text colours only cost headroom. Keep body text off glass wherever possible; Apple and Microsoft both put short labels and glyphs on glass and body text on opaque layers (I).

## Shape, spacing and type come straight from each platform

Windows 11 uses **8 px for top-level and transient surfaces** (windows, flyouts, dialogs), **4 px for in-page controls**, and 0 where edges touch ([Geometry in Windows 11](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/geometry)). Material 3's scale is 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 28, 32, 48 dp plus full ([ShapeTokens.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ShapeTokens.kt)). Windows spacing is a 4 px grid with 8 / 12 / 16 epx rhythm ([Content layout](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/basics/content-basics), [Fluent 2 Layout](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout)). The type ramp is Segoe UI Variable, Regular and Semibold only: Caption 12/16, Body 14/20, Body Large 18/24, Subtitle 20/28, Title 28/36 ([Typography in Windows](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/typography)). Android uses the M3 scale: Body L/M/S 16/24, 14/20, 12/16; Title L/M/S 22/28, 16/24, 14/20; Label L/M/S 14/20, 12/16, 11/16 ([TypeScaleTokens.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/TypeScaleTokens.kt)). The Android tokens are AndroidX implementation values, not the m3.material.io spec text, which was unreadable.

## Motion: published Android springs, published Windows curves, no Windows springs

Material 3 publishes spring tokens as damping ratio / stiffness. Expressive spatial: default 0.8 / 380, fast 0.6 / 800, slow 0.8 / 200. Standard spatial: default 0.9 / 700, fast 0.9 / 1400, slow 0.9 / 300. Effects springs for colour and opacity are critically damped in both: 1.0 / 1600 (default), 3800 (fast), 800 (slow) ([ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt), [StandardMotionTokens.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/StandardMotionTokens.kt), [Motion.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/theming/Motion.md)). Derived overshoot (D): 0.6 gives about 9.5%, 0.8 about 1.5%, 0.9 about 0.2%, so only "fast expressive" visibly bounces. Windows publishes no spring table; it publishes durations of 83 / 167 / 250 / 333 ms and curves: enter cubic-bezier(0,0,0,1) ("Direct Exit" is the same curve and must be paired with a fade), exit "Gentle Exit" (1,0,1,1) at 167 ms for position and scale only, point-to-point (0.55,0.55,0,1) ([Motion in Windows](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/motion), [Timing and easing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/motion/timing-and-easing)). Apple advises bounce 0 by default and under about 0.4 for UI ([WWDC23 10158 notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc23-10158-animate-with-springs/), U: secondary).

WCAG 2.3.3 (animation from interactions) is Level AAA, so reduced motion is a platform courtesy rather than an AA requirement ([Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Detect transparency, contrast and animation changes live, because all three can change while the tray app runs.

## One accent, amber as warning, and the 1.4.11 ruling

Fluent 2 says brand colour is for buttons, CTAs and selected states, warns that overuse "can dilute a hierarchy", and reserves red, yellow and green for status, "not for decoration" ([Fluent 2 color](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/color)). Android's own permission guidance marks a denied feature with "an error icon and color" at the affected spot ([Request runtime permissions](https://developer.android.com/training/permissions/requesting)). M3 has no warning or success role, so Relay defines an extended colour pair (colour, on-colour, container) itself; the "no official role" statement is inferred from search results, since the M3 colour page was unreadable. Yellow already means caution in Fluent, so amber as a second accent would collide with that meaning.

On 1.4.11, W3C states that visual information needed to identify a component needs 3:1 against adjacent colours; a text input whose only cue is its border needs a 3:1 border (the example uses #767676, and #AAA fails), while a control identified by other content such as text or a contrasting icon does not need a boundary ([Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Disabled controls are exempt; an author's choice of low contrast is not. Material encodes the split as `outline` (boundaries needing 3:1) and `outline-variant` (dividers) ([Compose ColorScheme](https://kotlinlang.org/api/compose-multiplatform/material3/androidx.compose.material3/-color-scheme/), U: secondary); Angular Components moved non-text elements from outline-variant to outline to pass ([PR 28995](https://fr.github.com/angular/components/pull/28995), U: secondary).

## Anti-AI-look checklist

No peer-reviewed or NN/g source defines an "AI look"; the evidence is practitioner consensus, so treat these as heuristics. The most checkable catalogue is Impeccable's ([anti-patterns](https://impeccable.style/anti-patterns)), corroborated by blogs listing the same tells: Inter/Roboto type, purple-blue gradients, centred hero, identical icon-heading-text card rows, emoji icons, decorative glass ([DEV](https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh), [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c); both seen only as snippets). One counter-view holds the real culprit is incoherence ([DEV](https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/why-ai-generated-uis-look-off-and-the-one-principle-that-fixes-it-4j20)), which supports token linting. Relay's checklist (items 1, 3 to 8 map to Impeccable; 2 (no emoji in chrome), 9 and 10 are ours):

1. No gradient text or gradient surfaces; no glow halos.
2. No emoji in chrome; one icon family per platform, one weight.
3. No card inside a card; prefer rows and lists to card grids.
4. No icon-tile above a heading; no side-stripe accent borders.
5. One elevation style per surface (border or shadow, not both).
6. Every spacing, radius and size value comes from the token scale; section gaps visibly larger than item gaps.
7. Glass only where it solves layering (flyout, sheet), never as decoration.
8. No marketing adjectives, placeholder names or repeated labels in copy.
9. Left-aligned content; status stated in words plus icon, not colour alone.
10. Every animation answers "what does this mean"; otherwise cut it.

| | Quick Share accept prompt ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/nearby-share-3197368/)); Windows UAC, Apple permission sheets (memory) | Recipient accepts each incoming transfer; name the requester, two buttons |

## Conclusion

The research shows that Relay's design risk lies in the glass layer, not in its tokens: every platform number it needs is published except the ones that make glass look good (border alpha, highlight), and every vendor has quietly retreated toward more opacity. Relay should therefore define each glass token as a triple (blurred, scrim-only, solid) and ship the solid state first, so that the "UI shows X but reality is Y" class of bug cannot arise from a silently missing blur. The same rule covers consent: the Android MediaProjection and Windows Focus Session findings imply that toasts can be suppressed and chips can stop sharing, so a pending or active share must be visible in persistent UI, with consent timing out to denied.

## Open gaps

- No published border alpha, inner highlight or tint-hue guidance for glass from any vendor; set by screenshot testing. Haze's Fluent numbers were not checked against WinUI source.
- The HIG Materials page, m3.material.io (shapes, elevation, spacing, motion, colour roles) and Fluent 2 sub-pages were unreadable; Android values come from AndroidX source, and the warning-role absence in M3 is inferred.
- No published Windows spring parameters; Apple's `.smooth`/`.snappy`/`.bouncy` values were not verified.
- Whether Compose springs honour animator duration scale 0 and whether any stock Android reduce-transparency flag exists are unconfirmed; Windows "Animation effects" naming is inferred from the SPI flag.
- Real-app evidence is thin for amber-as-warning, Pixel Settings radii, LocalSend, KDE Connect, Files by Google, Signal, Tailscale and UAC; those were listed from memory and need a screenshot pass.
- Exact hex for WinUI `SolidBackgroundFillColorBase` and `LayerFillColorDefault`, NavigationView compact pane widths, and Quick Settings flyout dimensions are not sourced.
- Material 3 1.5.0 stable had not shipped (rc01, 2026-11-04); motion token files may shift.
- Anti-AI-look sources are all opinion; no empirical study exists.
